How pricing works

The proposal names the route, the likely outcome and the handling, before anything is public.

What moves the final terms:

Category and demand. Fine art, furniture, jewelry and collectibles do not behave the same way in the market.

Value band and route. A direct purchase favours speed. Consignment favours the price. Brokerage suits a rare or high piece.

Documents and provenance. A clear history can improve a buyer’s confidence, and with it the price and the pace.

Condition and restoration. What has been repaired, and how well, shapes both the number and the buyer’s expectations.

Handling and logistics. Size, fragility and destination change the packing and the cost of moving the object.

Questions

What owners usually ask before sending photographs.

Do you guarantee authenticity?

We collect provenance where it exists and share the documents that belong to the object. Any limit on what we can say is stated in the proposal, not hidden under a general promise.

How do I know which route is right?

We look at the category, the condition, the provenance and the demand we can see, then recommend the route that fits the object and your priority — speed, price, or privacy. The options are explained before any next step.

How long does it take?

A first valuation is usually a matter of days. A sale takes longer, and depends on the route and the market. The proposal gives the timing we can stand behind for that object.

Can the whole matter stay private?

Yes. Valuations and sales can be handled without a public listing. Photographs are not circulated until you have agreed the terms.

Which documents help?

Certificates, invoices, exhibition records and any provenance you have. They are useful. They are not always required for a first look.

Do you work only in Dubai?

The company is in Meydan Free Zone, Dubai. Clients and buyers may be in the Gulf or further afield. Logistics are arranged when the object needs them.
